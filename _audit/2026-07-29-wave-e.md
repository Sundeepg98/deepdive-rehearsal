The argument each one repairs:

- **circuit-breaker** — `idempoten*` appears **zero** times in the file (counted, not trusted),
  while its write-fallback Follow says "durably enqueue the intent... and drain it when the
  dependency recovers." The calls that tripped the breaker are timed-out charges, so the drain
  re-executes a charge that may already have landed: the topic's own fallback converts an
  availability incident into a correctness one.
- **rate-limiting** — the card handled Redis being *down*, the clean case. The ambiguous case is
  one `INCR` timing out against a healthy Redis. `INCR` is not idempotent, so a library retry
  debits twice, and the direction is what bites: the client collects 429s for traffic it never
  sent, arriving as a support ticket rather than an alert.
- **debugging** — "replay the 30,000 failed requests" *is* the second incident. The Int2 splits
  the population by KNOWN outcome (a 4xx: replays safely) versus UNKNOWN (timeouts, 5xx on a
  write path: reconcile instead), and names the trade as swapping a loud outage for exactly the
  invisible wrongness the green dashboard taught this topic to distrust.
- **backpressure** — deadline-drop is free for reads and not free for writes. The check must
  precede the side effect, and a write path may only be shed if it was made repeatable first.
  The failure inverts the topic's own instrument: it moves neither goodput, latency, nor queue
  depth.

## 3. Pass 2 — the arithmetic-defense beat (S1)

### 3.1 Two additions, and the evidenced NO behind that number

I swept for the famous numbers: latency tables, C10K-class claims, power-of-two-choices,
availability nines and their composition, Little's Law, Bloom bits-per-element, HyperLogLog,
fan-out tail amplification, cache hit-ratio arithmetic, write-amplification T factors. **Nearly
every one already carries its defense, and I re-derived the arithmetic rather than trusting the
claim:**

- **slos** — 0.1% of 43,200 minutes = ~43 min, and "three hard 99.9% dependencies cap you at
  99.7%". Checked: 0.999³ = 0.99700. Correct.
- **probabilistic-structures** — 1.44 bits given as 1/ln 2 (= 1.4427), HLL at m = 2^14 as
  1.04/128 = 0.81%. Both correct.
- **caching** — already runs this exact beat, verbatim in spirit: *"You're at a 90 percent hit
  ratio and you push it to 95. How much does source load actually change? It **halves**. This is
  the thing people get backwards."*
- **backpressure, autoscaling, circuit-breaker, storage-engines, sharding-strategies,
  state-machine** — these are the donors themselves.

Two topics quoted a folklore constant with no defense attached. **Both are classes the dispatch
named explicitly**, which is a good sign the sweep's instinct was right even though its estimate
("every topic that quotes a folklore constant") was far too broad.

### 3.2 load-balancing — power-of-two-choices

The card states the balls-into-bins result precisely and **never produces a number**. The
misstatement is quoting `log log n` as **the load** when it is the **excess over the mean**:

- At n = 100, `log log n / log 2` ≈ **2** — so the candidate says "the busiest backend holds
  about two requests."
- A million requests over a hundred backends puts the **average at 10,000**. The real max is
  ~**10,002**. Wrong by a factor of about **5,000**.
- Correct heavily-loaded form is `m/n` **plus** that term — and the term **does not grow with m**.
- One choice has no constant at all: excess ≈ `sqrt(2 · (m/n) · ln n)` = `sqrt(2 · 10,000 · 4.605)`
  ≈ **303**, so ~**10,300**.
- Real headline: **the imbalance stops growing.** A ~3% win *at any volume*, not 3% today.

### 3.3 devices-dispatch — the C10K claim

The card says "real memory pressure" and quantifies nothing. The misstatement is multiplying by a
bare socket buffer — *"50,000 × 4 KB = 200 MB, that's nothing."* C10K solved the **event loop**
(epoll makes idle descriptors nearly free); it left the **per-connection buffers**, which TLS
dominates at ~16 KB per direction — so ~**32 KB/connection** and ~**1.6 GB** for 50,000 before a
byte of application state. That is what `ssl_buffer_size` exists to tune.

Both landed as a third `Follow:` exactly where the constant is quoted, plus one arithmetic-tell
clause on each card's `Senior:`.

- **A green ratchet is not a claim that the added beats are GOOD.** `bank_pushback` measures
  vocabulary novelty and `bank_novelty` measures contiguity; neither can judge whether a pushback
  aims at the seam its Model opens, or whether the answer is correct. Both checks say so in their
  own headers. **Ten new exchanges and beats were authored here and a human verifier has not yet
  read one of them** — that is the next agent's job, and it is the load-bearing one.
- **The arithmetic in §3 is mine and should be re-derived, not trusted.** I showed the work for
  exactly that reason. The load-balancing figures in particular rest on the heavily-loaded
  balanced-allocations result (excess independent of m), which is a stronger and less commonly
  quoted claim than the n-balls-into-n-bins form the card already states.
- **Pass 2 is two topics, and that is a judgment, not a sweep.** I claim the corpus already
  defends its folklore constants nearly everywhere and that these were the two genuine gaps. A
  reader who disagrees should re-run the scan in §3.1 rather than assume coverage.
- **The three dispatched inventory agents never returned.** Every list here is my own
  measurement. A cold second pass over the pass-3 funnel (326 → 45 → 3) would be the highest-value
  independent check, because that funnel's last step is pure judgment.
