  ratio and you push it to 95. How much does source load actually change? It **halves**. This is

Two topics quoted a folklore constant with no defense attached. **Both are classes the dispatch

### 3.2 load-balancing — power-of-two-choices

The card states the balls-into-bins result precisely and **never produces a number**. The
misstatement is quoting `log log n` as **the load** when it is the **excess over the mean**:

- At n = 100, `log log n / log 2` ≈ **2** — so the candidate says "the busiest backend holds
  about two requests."
- A million requests over a hundred backends puts the **average at 10,000**. The real max is
  ~**10,002**. Wrong by a factor of about **5,000**.
- Correct heavily-loaded form is `m/n` **plus** that term — and the term **does not grow with m**.
- One choice has no constant at all: excess ≈ `sqrt(2 · (m/n) · ln n)` = `sqrt(2 · 10,000 · 4.605)`
  ≈ **303**, so ~**10,300**.
- Real headline: **the imbalance stops growing** — as an **absolute** claim. Two choices provision
  the mean plus a *constant* at any scale; one choice buys headroom that never stops growing.
  The **percentage** moves the other way (see §9): ~3% at 10k requests/backend, ~0.3% at 1M.

### 3.3 devices-dispatch — the C10K claim

The card says "real memory pressure" and quantifies nothing. The misstatement is multiplying by a
bare socket buffer — *"50,000 × 4 KB = 200 MB, that's nothing."* C10K solved the **event loop**
(epoll makes idle descriptors nearly free); it left the **per-connection buffers**, which TLS
dominates at ~16 KB per direction — so ~**32 KB/connection** and ~**1.6 GB** for 50,000 before a
byte of application state. That is what `ssl_buffer_size` exists to tune.

Both landed as a third `Follow:` exactly where the constant is quoted, plus one arithmetic-tell
clause on each card's `Senior:`.

## 9. The bounce, and what it corrected (re-freeze)

wE-verifier read the freeze and returned **one blocking finding and two one-word nits**. The
content otherwise held: all three pass-3 Int2s judged true and seam-aimed, the pass-3 funnel
reproduced row-for-row, and every skip I recorded in §2.1 and §4 verified as correct.

**The blocking one was mine, and it is the most instructive thing in this report.** The P2C
Follow closed on *"the win is 3% at **any** volume rather than 3% today."* That is **false**, and
it is refuted by the sentence immediately before it in my own paragraph. The one-choice excess
grows like `sqrt(m/n)` while the mean grows like `m/n`, so the **percentage** win *shrinks* as
traffic rises. I had conflated *"the two-choice excess is a constant"* — true, and stated
correctly one sentence earlier — with *"the percentage gap is a constant"*, which does not
follow. That is precisely the class of arithmetic misstatement the card exists to teach against,
committed inside the card itself, in the wave whose entire job was propagating that pattern.

The replacement is the true and stronger claim, with the scaling rule that makes it stick: **every
hundredfold increase in volume divides the percentage by ten and multiplies the absolute excess by
ten** (~3% at 10k requests/backend, ~0.3% at 1M; excess 303.5 → 3034.9). So quoting the percentage
talks you out of the algorithm exactly as it begins earning its keep, and the claim worth making
is the absolute one. Everything else in that paragraph survived independent re-derivation by both
of us — 2.20, ~10,002, the ~5,000x, and 303.5.

**The "arithmetic disagreement" I reported in the first re-freeze DID NOT EXIST, and that
retraction belongs here rather than in a message.** Working from the bounce summary alone, I
inferred the verifier's 21.7% at `m/n = 100` came from `sqrt((m/n)·ln n)` with the factor of 2
dropped, and I said so in the re-freeze commit and to the team lead. Reading their actual
cold-verify (`_audit/2026-07-29-wave-e-coldverify.md` §1.1) shows I misread their table twice:
its `m` column is **total requests**, not `m/n`, and its percentage is taken **over the one-choice
peak**, not over the mean. Recomputing with my own formula reproduces their table row for row —
peaks 130.3 / 10,303.5 / 1,003,034.9 against their 130.3 / 10,303 / 1,003,035, and wins of
21.6% / 2.92% / 0.30% against their 21.7% / 2.9% / 0.30%. **We use the identical formula and agree
on every figure.** The only difference was the denominator, and at both points the card actually
prints (~3% and ~0.3%) the two denominators agree to the rounding shown.

The lesson is the same one this section already records, one level up: I diagnosed a disagreement
from a summary instead of reading the primary evidence, and published the diagnosis. The card was
never affected — its numbers are correct under either denominator — but the freeze report carried
a false claim about a teammate's arithmetic until this correction.

**One real refinement did come out of it.** The scaling rule now reads "multiplies the absolute
excess by ten and cuts the percentage by *roughly* the same factor". The absolute factor is exactly
ten (√100); the percentage factor is only asymptotically ten, because the denominator grows too —
the verifier's own table shows 21.7 → 2.9 (a factor of 7.5) before it settles to 2.9 → 0.30 (9.7).
