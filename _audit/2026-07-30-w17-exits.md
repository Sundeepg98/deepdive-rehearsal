## X3 -- a topic picked from `#home` switched the registry and moved nobody

### What was actually wrong

Not the router. `Router.setTopic` early-returns on a topic-less route (`TOPICLESS = {home:1}`,
`router.js:34`, `:126`) because a topic switch used to rewrite `#home` into `#saga/home` via
`replaceState` and destroy the home's own history entry, killing Back. That guard is correct, its
two source comments both say "Measured", and **it is untouched by this change.**

The missing piece was the **caller**. `HomeView.bind`'s `onPick` compensated by navigating; the two
overlays that also pick topics had no equivalent:

| pick surface | pre-fix behaviour |
|---|---|
| home library card (`Panels.bind` -> `HomeView.onPick`) | navigates -- correct |
| Search overlay (`search-overlay.js:409 navigateTo`) | `TopicRegistry.setTopic` only -- **lost** |
| Topic index (`index-overlay.js:57 onPick: close`) | closes the panel only -- **lost** |

### Before -> after, measured

```

                         topic:"content-pipeline", sideId:"Content Pipeline",

```

Internals TOPIC Messaging & Events - the distributed log"}`):

         sideId:"Kafka Internals", homeVisible:true, appVisible:false}

         topic:"kafka-internals", sideId:"Kafka Internals",

### The fix

One rule, one implementation: **`LastVisit.navigateAfterPick(kind)`** in `src/scripts/app/last-visit.js`
-- the module that already owns both halves the rule needs (the `nav.last` resume pointer, and the
"a topic-less route is not a topic" test `record()` has always made). It skips `'hash'` picks (those
assign `location.hash` themselves; navigating too would push a junk entry and break Back), no-ops on
a topic route, and otherwise navigates to `LastVisit.resumeView()`.

All three pick surfaces now route through it, and `home-view.js`'s private `lastView()` copy is gone:
**three copies of a rule is how two of them came to be forgotten.** Net source delta is 5 files,
**+65/-12** (`git diff --numstat d481901 HEAD -- src/`).

Two ordering details that are load-bearing, both documented at the call site:

- **It fires BEFORE `setTopic`**, matching the order `Panels.bind` already fires `onPick` in. The
  navigate pushes one entry carrying the outgoing topic's prefix and `setTopic`'s silent
  `replaceState` re-prefixes it, so the switch costs no second history entry.
- **It must not depend on `setTopic` having succeeded.** `setTopic` early-returns on `id === cur` --
  which is exactly the pick a returning user makes most often, the topic they left. A fix that
  navigated only after a successful switch would be silently dead on that path. Guarded by a plant.

### Guard -- `test/search_deadend.cjs`, +10 arms (5 in round 1, 5 more in round 2)

The file previously walked only the zero-hit whole-system-prompt path, from a topic route. Added:
the two home pick paths, plus three plants. The home arms assert the **whole landing** -- hash AND
title AND view AND `.app` visibility -- because the pre-fix build already moved the topic, so any
arm reading only the topic (or only "the hash changed") would have passed on it. The landing hash is
compared against the **canonical encoding** recomputed in-page (boot topic bare, every other topic
prefixed) rather than a literal, so it stays true when the content set changes.

**Watched RED on the pre-fix build -- 5 of 5 home arms failed, both topic-route plants stayed GREEN**
(so they are a control, not a symptom):

The two green plants are the **anti-overreach** arms. **The first version of this report claimed they
catch "the obvious wrong fix -- navigate unconditionally". They do not, and the cold verify proved
it.** "Navigate unconditionally" has two readings, and the plants only reach one:

| the over-fix | what it does | these two plants |
|---|---|---|
| **hardcoded destination** (`Router.navigate('walk')` regardless of the pointer) -- verifier's `plant-a3` | moves the user somewhere visibly wrong | **RED, as advertised** |
| **guard deletion** (drop `rc.view !== 'home'`, keep `resumeView()`) -- verifier's `plant-a2` | navigates from every route | **GREEN on all 24 arms** |

And guard deletion is a **real regression**, not a stylistic one -- the verifier receipted it: deep-link
to `#event-driven/wb` with a resume pointer still reading `drill`, then pick a topic in the index.
The shipped build keeps you in the whiteboard (`#kafka-internals/wb`); the mutant dumps you into the
pointer's view (`#kafka-internals/drill`).

**Why no interaction arm can see it, which is the part worth remembering.** `LastVisit.record()`
re-syncs `nav.last.view` on every routechange, so on any *settled* topic route `resumeView()` has
already converged on the view you are in -- the mutant's extra navigate is a no-op, identical in hash
*and* in `history.length` (`3 -> 3` on both builds). The divergence exists only in the ~500ms window
before `LastVisit` arms. A driver could only reach it by racing that window, and a check whose redness
depends on winning a race will eventually go green by luck.

**Closed in round 2 by the E arms** -- see the addendum. They drop to the layer where the property is
timeless: `navigateAfterPick` is a predicate, and it must *refuse* off the home route.

## X4 -- the keyboard-shortcuts overlay clipped 208px of itself, keyboard-unreachable

### What was actually wrong

`#keybody` was the only one of the app's four `.cram-body` dialog scroll regions shipped without
`tabindex="0" role="region"` and a name. Measured census of every `[role=dialog][aria-modal=true]`
on the pre-fix build -- read from the DOM, not from a list:

| dialog | body | tabindex | role | accessible name |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `cramov` | `#cram` | `"0"` | `region` | Cram sheet content |
| `scopeov` | `#scopebody` | `"0"` | `region` | Scope-it content |
| `planov` | `#planbody` | `"0"` | `region` | Game plan content |
| **`keyov`** | **`#keybody`** | **`null`** | **`null`** | **`null`** |

### Before -> after, measured (overlay opened with a real `?`)

| measurement | pre-fix | post-fix |
|---|---|---|
| `#keybody.scrollHeight / clientHeight` | 911 / 703 | 911 / 703 |
| clipped px | 208 | 208 |
| `tabindex` / `role` / `aria-label` | `null` / `null` / `null` | `"0"` / `region` / `Keyboard shortcuts content` |
| distinct stops over 12 real `Tab` presses | **1** (`button#keyx`) | **2** (`div#keybody`, `button#keyx`) |
| presses to reach the body | never | **1** |
| `scrollTop` after real `ArrowDown`, **settled** | 0 | **40** |
| `scrollTop` after real `PageDown`, **settled** | 0 | **208 = max** |
| `scrollTop` after real `End` | **0** | **208 = max** |
| leaf nodes not fully visible at rest, unreachable | **10** | **0** |

**Three numbers in this table were corrected in round 2** (the originals were `13`, `184` and `11`).
The first two were **mid-animation samples**: Chromium animates keyboard scrolling here -- `.cram-body`
sets no `scroll-behavior`, so the animation is the browser's own -- and reading once after two rAFs
catches it in flight. Polled to rest, one `ArrowDown` gives the sequence `0, 10, 24, 36, 40, 40, ...`
and `PageDown` gives `0, 18, 79, 208, 208, ...`; the original figures appear inside those sequences as
intermediates. Re-measured independently at `1280x800` in round 2, reproducing the verifier's values.

**The fold count and its convention.** `#keybody` occupies rect top 78 / bottom 781, so "the fold" is
y=781. The original `11` matched no convention; the honest figure depends on which you pick, so it is
stated rather than implied:

| convention | count |
|---|---|
| **leaf nodes not fully visible (`bottom > 781`)** -- the one this report means | **10** |
| leaf nodes strictly below the fold (`top >= 781`) | 8 |
| all text-bearing elements, not fully visible | 21 |
| all text-bearing elements, strictly below | 15 |

The loose convention is the one the prose implies, because `G  Start the guided tour` is *partially*
clipped rather than fully below -- it counts as unreachable content but fails the strict test. The 10
are exactly the rows the audit named: `G` / `Start the guided tour`, `D` / `Cycle spacing density`,
**`Esc` / `Close any open panel`**, **`?` / `Bring up this list`**, and the `Ctrl` / command-key
footer. Two of them document the keys that open and close this very panel. The qualitative claim is
unchanged and was right either way; only the arithmetic was loose.

### The fix

One attribute triple on `src/overlays/keyboard.html:7`, matching the three siblings' exact idiom
including their `"<dialog name> content"` naming voice:

```html
<div class="cram-body" id="keybody" tabindex="0" role="region" aria-label="Keyboard shortcuts content">
```

### Guard -- `test/overlay_keyboard.cjs`, +9 arms (a scroll-region CENSUS)

**The hole was in the existing check's own denominator.** Its contract is "every focusable element
in the dialog is reachable" -- compared against the dialog's true focusable set, deliberately, so it
would not false-positive on a dialog whose only control legitimately is Close. `#keybody` was not
focusable, so it was never in that set, so the check reported `[keyboard] Tab reaches EVERY focusable
in the dialog (1: button#keyx)` -- **PASS**, correctly, on a dialog that was clipping 208px.

The census closes the **class**, not the instance: it enumerates every dialog scroll body from the
DOM and requires each to be focusable, `role=region`, named, and a real overflow container -- so a
fifth overlay is covered the day it lands, and no future omission can hide behind a focusable set it
was excluded from. Two properties keep it from being decoration:

- **Its own negative control.** It asserts the census is non-empty and covers every dialog that has
  a body (`census.length >= 4 && census.length === dialogsWithBody`). A selector that silently
  matched nothing would otherwise report a spotless sweep of zero elements.
- **The functional arm proves the premise first.** It asserts `#keybody` genuinely overflows before
  demanding that the keyboard scroll it, so the scroll assertions cannot pass for free at a viewport
  where nothing is clipped.

The accessible name is read through **two independent instruments** -- the attribute, and the real
accessibility tree via `getByRole('region', {name: /keyboard shortcuts/i})` -- so a stray
`aria-hidden` cannot satisfy both.

**Watched RED on the pre-fix build -- 7 of 9 new arms failed; the two that passed are the census's
own negative control and the overflow premise, exactly as designed:**

```
PASS [census] the scroll-region census actually found the scroll regions (>=4, one per dialog that has one)
FAIL [census] EVERY dialog scroll region is focusable (tabindex="0") -- #cram, #keybody, #scopebody, #planbody
  -> not focusable: #keybody in #keyov (tabindex null) -- its content is unreachable by keyboard
     the moment it overflows
FAIL [census] EVERY dialog scroll region declares role="region"      -> missing role=region: #keybody
FAIL [census] EVERY dialog scroll region carries an accessible name  -> unnamed: #keybody
PASS [census] ...and each really is an overflow container
PASS [keyboard] #keybody genuinely overflows, so the arms below are not vacuous (108px below the fold)
FAIL [keyboard] Tab REACHES the shortcuts body itself, not just its close button
  -> the ring was button#keyx -- the body is not a tab stop, so the clipped rows have no keyboard path at all
FAIL [keyboard] ArrowDown on the focused body SCROLLS it
FAIL [keyboard] End reaches the BOTTOM, so every clipped row is keyboard-reachable
  -> End left 108px still unreachable
FAIL [keyboard] the body resolves as a NAMED region in the accessibility tree
  -> {"regionsInKeyov":0,"matchedByAccessibleName":0}
OVERLAY KEYBOARD: FAIL  (7 of 57 assertions)
```

(That run is at this check's own `1440x900` viewport, where the clip is 108px rather than 208px --
the arm measures the overflow rather than asserting a literal, which is why it holds at both sizes.)

Post-fix: `OVERLAY KEYBOARD: PASS (57 assertions across 7 dialogs)`. The pre-existing arm absorbed
the fix on its own and now reads `[keyboard] Tab reaches EVERY focusable in the dialog (2:
button#keyx, div#keybody)`.

## One thing a reviewer should look at

`home-view.js` was touched although the brief named only the two overlays. Its private `lastView()`
and its inline `onPick` navigate were replaced by calls to the shared `LastVisit` rule. This is
deliberate -- leaving it would have kept a third copy of the rule whose duplication is the reason
two surfaces shipped without it -- and it is behaviour-preserving: that callback only ever fires
while the home is the route, which is precisely the condition `navigateAfterPick` tests, and
`resumeView()` is a literal move of `lastView()`'s body. It is covered by VR (its
`lastViewTitle()` renders into the home's Resume card, and all 16 baselines matched) and by the
`scoreboard_resume`, `visual_pane_smoke` and `room_browser` checks, all green. If the reviewer wants
the change surface minimal rather than the rule single-sourced, reverting just this file to its base
version restores the old private copy without affecting either fix.
