**Builder:** w18-builder - 2026-07-30

## 0. HEADLINE

**Two of the three defects are not WebKit bugs, and that is the load-bearing finding of this wave.**

The audit filed X5 as "engine-attributable -- Chromium has all 7 chips in the *first* sample". Driving
it here says otherwise: **the race is present in Chromium too, with an identical mechanism and an
identical one-frame width.** Only the *cost* of that frame is engine-specific. X8 was already known
engine-independent and is confirmed. So of the three, **only X2a is genuinely WebKit-attributable**,
and the other two are guarded in the gate's own engine by a new check rather than filed as an engine
difference nothing can hold.

| finding | engine-attributable? | before | after | guarded by |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **X2a** seg scrollbar band | **YES** (WebKit only) | WK `73/60`, gutter 13; taps 38px effective | WK `61/60`, gutter 1; taps 44px | VR byte-identity (no new check) |
| **X5** jump-strip race | **NO** -- same in Chromium | 1 racing frame, 44px relayout, BOTH engines | 0 frames, 0px, BOTH engines | `cram_fit` (new) |
| **X8** panel overflow | **NO** -- byte-identical in CR | 18px below the fold, both orientations | 18px of clearance | `cram_fit` (new) |

## 1. X2a -- WebKit reserved AND painted a 12px scrollbar inside the fixed phone nav

**Fix:** `src/styles.css` -- `scrollbar-width:none` folded into the existing `.sidebar .seg` rule, plus
`.sidebar .seg::-webkit-scrollbar{display:none}` beside it, both inside the same `@media(max-width:919px)`
block that already declares the strip's `overflow-x:auto`.

### Placement -- a deliberate deviation from the brief, recorded

The brief said "beside the existing `.cram-jump` pair at `styles.css:2125` (mirror that pair's exact
idiom and comment voice)". **The idiom is mirrored exactly; the placement is not.** The rule is at the
`.sidebar .seg` declaration (`:801`/`:821`) rather than ~1370 lines away in the cram block, because the
suppression belongs with the `overflow-x:auto` that creates the scrollbar -- anyone editing that
overflow now sees the suppression in the same breath. **Behaviour is identical either way:** both
candidate homes are inside `@media(max-width:919px)` blocks, the selector and specificity are unchanged,
and there is no competing declaration between them. Verified in the built artifact, not just asserted.

### Why both declarations, when the brief said one line

`.cram-jump` -- the analogous horizontal chip strip, and the audit's own reference for "the exact shape"
-- ships **both**. `scrollbar-width:none` is measured to do **nothing** in WebKit 26.5 (the audit's
injection table, reproduced here); the pseudo is what does the work. It is carried for the day WebKit
implements the standard property, so the app is not left depending on a non-standard pseudo-element
forever. It is a no-op today in all three engines, which the VR result (S5) is the acceptance test for.
Note the app has both idioms already: `.sidebar` (`:45`) ships the pseudo alone.

### Receipts -- 390x844, dpr 1, `iw`/`ih` asserted on every read

| measurement | **WK before** | **WK after** | CR before | CR after |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `.sidebar .seg` bounding height | **73.0** | **61.0** | 61.0 | 61.0 |
| `offsetHeight / clientHeight` | **73 / 60** | **61 / 60** | 61 / 60 | 61 / 60 |
| gutter (`offsetH - clientH`) | **13** (1px border + 12px bar) | **1** (border only) | 1 | 1 |
| computed `scrollbar-width` | `auto` | `none` | `auto` | `none` |
| `scrollWidth / clientWidth` | 977 / 390 | 977 / 390 | 976 / 390 | 976 / 390 |
| `#homeBtn` **EFFECTIVE** tap height | **38** of 44 | **44** | 44 | 44 |
| `#tntrigger` **EFFECTIVE** tap height | **38** of 44 | **44** | 44 | 44 |

The strip still genuinely overflows after the fix (`scrollWidth 977 > clientWidth 390`) -- the strip
scrolls, it just no longer bills the band for a scrollbar. **The 6px of occlusion matches the audit's
`6.0px / 13.6% of 44px` exactly.**

**Chromium neutrality: the geometry is unchanged in every column above.** The only Chromium-visible
delta is the computed `scrollbar-width` string going `auto -> none`, which moves no pixel at rest (CR
already reserved 0 here, and the audit's dpr1 decode showed it paints nothing in that band at rest).

### Instrument limitation, stated rather than papered over

My probe's `elementFromPoint(homeBtn.cx, segTop + 2)` column returns `DIV.seg` in **both** engines, where
the audit reported `BUTTON.tn-step tn-home` for Chromium. The sample lands 2px into the strip's own 8px
top padding, above the tab buttons in any engine, so **that column does not discriminate in my
instrument and is not offered as evidence.** The discriminating measurements are the gutter and the
effective tap heights, and both match the audit's numbers exactly.

### Guard

**None added, by design** -- and the brief asked this to be said explicitly. VR byte-identity across the
16 committed baselines is the check: this is a change whose entire claim is "nothing moves in Chromium",
and the baselines are the instrument that already asks that question. See S5.

## 2. X5 -- the jump strip painted one frame behind the sections it indexes

**Fix:** `src/scripts/app/cram-sheet.js` -- the bounded rAF retry is replaced by a `MutationObserver` on
`<deep-cram>`'s shadow root. `buildCramJump` now builds immediately if sections exist, else observes;
`renderCramJump` is the extracted builder; `stopCramJumpWatch` disconnects on build and on `closeCram`.

### The mechanism is sharper than "WebKit frames are slow", and it changes the fix

The audit attributed this to frame cadence under a heavy synchronous render. That is real but it is the
*amplifier*, not the cause. **The rAF retry could never win, by specification ordering:**

- `<deep-cram>` renders from an **IntersectionObserver** callback (`cram-overlay.js:45-48`).
- In the HTML "update the rendering" steps, **animation-frame callbacks run BEFORE intersection
  observations are delivered.**
- So every retry sampled the shadow root in the frame *just ahead* of the render that would fill it, and
  the strip was **structurally guaranteed** to paint one frame behind its own sections.

**Measured, and it is exactly one frame in BOTH engines** -- which is the receipt that this is ordering,
not speed:

| rAF trace, pre-fix (`a84d68a`), 390x844, real trusted tap on `#cramopen` (hit `SPAN.mb-d`) | |
|---|---|
| **WebKit 26.5** | `t=483ms open sec=0 chips=0 stripH=16.3` -> `t=583ms sec=7 chips=0 stripH=16.8` -> `t=668ms sec=7 chips=7 stripH=60.8` |
| **Chromium 149** | `t=333ms open sec=0 chips=0 stripH=16.3` -> `t=445ms sec=7 chips=0 stripH=16.4` -> `t=467ms sec=7 chips=7 stripH=60.1` |

One frame of "sections on screen, index empty" in each. What differs is only what that frame **costs**
while a 7-section, 7619px sheet renders synchronously under a `backdrop-filter` over a 12MB document:
**~30ms in Chromium, ~140ms in WebKit**, where it reads as the sheet lurching under the reader.

### Mechanism chosen: MutationObserver. Both options weighed, per the brief.

| | **MutationObserver on the shadow root (CHOSEN)** | build from `deriveCram`'s output |
|---|---|---|
| timing | microtask at the end of the render's own task -- same frame, before paint | by construction, no dependence on render at all |
| W2's invariant ("it reads the sheet, it does not describe it") | **kept** -- chips are still the titles the sheet actually rendered | **traded away** -- a second list that can drift from a per-topic corpus |
| cost | one observer, disconnected on build | a second derive of the heaviest content in the app |

The W2 builder chose rendered-DOM deliberately, and the source says why: `deriveCram` emits a section
only when its slice has content, so a corpus that authors different sections per topic can never drift
out of sync with its own index. **The observer buys the timing without spending that**, so there was
nothing to trade -- the derive-source option is strictly worse here, not a close call. It would only win
if the render itself were unobservable, which it is not.

### Receipts -- before -> after, one instrument, both engines

Instrument: an rAF recorder armed **before** the open (so it is registered ahead of anything the app
schedules and runs first every frame), reduced to two structural facts. **Travel is read in LAYOUT space
(`#cram.offsetTop`) -- no transform can move it.**

| | **WK before** | **WK after** | **CR before** | **CR after** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| frames showing sections with an **empty** strip | **1** | **0** | **1** | **0** |
| chips present at the sections' **first paint** | **0** | **7** | **0** | **7** |
| body relayout (`#cram.offsetTop` travel) | **44px** | **0px** | **44px** | **0px** |
| layout offsets observed | `[88, 132]` | `[132]` | `[88, 132]` | `[132]` |

Post-fix WK trace: `t=580ms open sec=0 chips=0` -> `t=750ms sec=7 chips=7 stripH=60.8` -- **the sections
and their index arrive in the same frame**, so nothing the reader could already have been reading moves.

### Two discarded instruments, recorded so nobody re-derives them as measured

1. **Viewport-absolute section top.** Scored 4 (WK) / 12 (CR) distinct positions for a sheet that shifts
   once -- that was `panelIn` (`translateY(28px) scale(.96) -> none`) moving everything every frame.
2. **Panel-relative section top.** Subtracting the panel's own top cancels the translate but **not the
   scale**: a section 183px down the panel reads 176px at `scale(.96)`.

A third attempt -- excluding frames with a running animation -- then reported the **pre-fix build CLEAN**,
because the race resolves *during* the entrance. **That was a check that could not fail, and it was
caught only by running it against `a84d68a` before trusting it.** The final instrument keeps the two
metrics on two populations: the frame count sees every frame; the travel is layout-space.

### Out of scope, found while here (NOT fixed, filed for the record)

A topic switch **while the sheet is open** re-renders `<deep-cram>`'s shadow root, but the strip is not
rebuilt -- `buildCramJump` runs only from `openCram`. The chips' captured `secs` element references then
point into detached DOM, so a chip click would scroll against a stale rect. The audit only established
that switch-then-**reopen** is clean, which it is. This predates W18 and the fix here does not change it
(the observer disconnects after building, preserving today's one-shot semantics exactly). Deliberately
not widened into this wave.

## 3. X8 -- the cram panel was 18px taller than the space it was given

**Fix:** `src/styles.css` -- the `@media(max-width:919px)` override `max-height:100vh` ->
`max-height:calc(100vh - var(--space-36))`, matching the desktop rule. `var(--space-36)` is `.cram-ov`'s
own 18px inset doubled, which is why the two stay in step.

| 390x844 | before | after |
|---|---|---|
| panel rect | `[18, 862]` h **844** | `[18, 826]` h **808** |
| computed `max-height` | `843.999939px` (WK) / `844px` (CR) | `807.999939px` (WK) / `808px` (CR) |
| bottom vs `innerHeight` 844 | **+18px below the fold** | **-18px (clearance)** |

| 844x390 (landscape) | before | after |
|---|---|---|
| panel rect | `[18, 408]` h 390 | fits |
| bottom vs `innerHeight` 390 | **+18px below the fold** | within viewport |

**Byte-identical in Chromium before and after** -- confirmed both directions, so this is a gate blind
spot, not a WebKit difference. Measured **at rest**: `panelIn`'s animations awaited *and* consecutive
agreeing samples required. An early sample reads the panel at `top 35.9 / h 822.4` (scale ~0.974) and
would have scored the defect at 14px instead of 18 -- the `touch_floor` trap, hit and corrected here.

## 4. GUARDS

### New check: `test/cram_fit.cjs` -- registered in `check_all.py` (**68 -> 69**)

Owns X5 and X8 **in Chromium**, because both reproduce there. 13 assertions:

- **fit arm**, 390x844 and 844x390: panel bottom within the viewport, and the panel seated at the
  overlay's top inset (so "it fits" cannot be satisfied by a shrunken panel).
- **first-open arm**, on a **virgin page** (the race is first-open-only; a reopen finds its sections
  already rendered and is clean on the pre-fix build too, so reusing a page would assert nothing):
  zero frames may show sections with an empty index, and the body's layout offset may not travel.
- a **zero-section pass is not a pass** arm.

**The assertions are frame-relative and layout-space, never a millisecond budget** -- there is nothing
here to retune on a slower CI runner, which is what makes a first-open race safe to guard at all.

### Both arms plant, and both plants ABORT rather than fail an arm

| plant | effect | result |
|---|---|---|
| restore `max-height:100vh` | panel returns below the fold | **fires** in both orientations |
| defer the strip's `MutationObserver` by one animation frame (scoped to `<deep-cram>`'s root) -- i.e. reconstruct the pre-fix timing exactly | the race returns | **fires: 1 racing frame, 44px relayout** -- the exact pre-fix signature |

### X2a: no new guard, deliberately

already asks it. A `.seg.offsetHeight === clientHeight + border` assertion **run in Chromium would pass
on the pre-fix build** -- it can only earn its keep in WebKit, and the whole gate is Chromium-only. That
is W-X2b's problem to solve properly, not something to fake here with a check that cannot fail.

## 5. `grade_reveal` HARDENING

**Failing arm (2 occurrences on record, both rerun-clean):** *"reveal: a real click on Missed at the
reveal moment records the grade (level 1) under the probe content id"* -- 2026-07-29 residuals train,
2026-07-30 W16 train.

### Diagnosis

The arm read `#jm`'s rect **once**, via `BTN_BOX`, after `scrollTo`'s two rAFs -- then fired
`page.mouse.click` at those coordinates **blind**. Two load-scaled ways that sample goes stale between
the read and the click:

1. **The judge row arrives under the shared `pop` keyframe.** A rect read mid-animation is the animation,
   not the control -- `touch_floor`'s own header records this trap reading 43.2px for a 44px button.
2. **`scrollIntoView`'s layout can lag the settle** -- precisely the lag that made the *sibling*
   reachability arm flake at `[360]` and got it converted to a poll (`reachProbe` / `assertReachable`).

Either way the click misses, no grade is recorded, and the arm fails reading `undefined !== 1`. **The
check already knew this pattern was unsafe and had fixed it for the reachability arm; the functional
click arm was simply never given the same treatment.** That asymmetry is the whole defect.

### The change, before -> after semantics (explicit, for tracing)

| | **before** | **after** |
|---|---|---|
| precondition on the click | a rect existed (`!!missBox`) | the button is **at rest** (two consecutive agreeing samples) **and** two-sided reachable (`document.elementFromPoint` inside `#drill` **and** `shadowRoot.elementFromPoint` on `#jm`) |
| click coordinates | from a sample taken before the click, no reachability proof | from **the same sample that proved reachability** |
| the recorded-grade assertion | `recMiss.cards[id0] === 1 && recMiss.done === 1` | **byte-identical, unchanged** |
| the record poll | `waitForFunction` level 1, `.catch(() => {})` | **unchanged** |
| failure reporting | `hadButton: false` | the last probe state (why it was never reachable) |

**No assertion is weakened.** The recorded-level assertion is untouched; the click precondition is
**strictly stronger** than before. The dead `BTN_BOX` helper is removed (its only call site was this arm).

**The watched RED is preserved:** on a pre-fix deliverable `#jm` does not exist at the reveal stage, so
the poll times out and the arm is still red -- it now names the reason instead of missing silently.

### Stability tally -- 6 standalone runs, mixed load

Phase A is three sequential runs on a quiet box. Phase B is three **concurrent** runs that load each
other, which is the closest available proxy for the loaded CI runner both recorded failures happened on.
The ~2x runtime in phase B is the receipt that the load was real and not nominal.

| run | load | wall | result | assertions |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| q1 | quiet | 5s | **PASS** | 13 PASS / 0 FAIL |
| q2 | quiet | 5s | **PASS** | 13 PASS / 0 FAIL |
| q3 | quiet | 6s | **PASS** | 13 PASS / 0 FAIL |
| L1 | 3x concurrent | 11s | **PASS** | 13 PASS / 0 FAIL |
| L2 | 3x concurrent | 12s | **PASS** | 13 PASS / 0 FAIL |
| L3 | 3x concurrent | 11s | **PASS** | 13 PASS / 0 FAIL |

**6/6 PASS.** All six ran the **full** 13 assertions -- no run silently covered less -- and the
previously-flaky arm ("a real click on Missed at the reveal moment records the grade") passed in every
one, under load included. Plus the 7th run inside the gate itself (S6).

**Honest limit:** six passes cannot prove a race is gone; they show it did not reproduce under a load
that doubled the check's runtime. The argument that it is *fixed* rests on the mechanism -- the click is
now gated on the same at-rest + two-sided-reachability proof the sibling arm has used since it stopped
flaking -- not on the tally.

## 8. WHAT THIS WAVE DID NOT DO

- **X2b (the measured bar).** The hardcoded 56/69/88 and landscape 51/59/76 are untouched. They remain
  ~5px wrong in Chromium today, and X2a does not change that -- it removes the 12px WebKit *added* on
  top. X2b owns the `m-walk-*` re-baseline; this wave deliberately produced none.
- **Real macOS/iOS Safari.** Every WebKit number here is Playwright's WebKit 26.5 **Windows** build,
  which uses classic space-reserving scrollbars. Default iOS uses overlay scrollbars and may show X2a as
  0. What is proven is that it is not an emulation artifact, and that the suppression is free where the
  defect is absent.
- **The topic-switch-while-open staleness** described in S2 -- found, filed, not fixed.
- **WebKit in the gate.** The gate is still Chromium-only. X5 and X8 are now guarded there because they
  reproduce there; X2a is not, and cannot be, until something runs WebKit in CI.
- **No merge, no push, no change to `master` or any sibling worktree.**
